# M6 — Proposed dispositions for the 30 undeclared legacy routes

**Date:** 2026-08-19 · **For:** the backend contract owner
**Source:** `docs/integration/M6_UNDECLARED_LEGACY_ROUTES.md`, regenerable from
`tool/integration_probe`.

Each route the customer app calls needs an entry in `V1_CONTRACT[].legacy`, or
it has no successor, no disposition and no place on the deprecation clock. 94
routes already carry `Deprecation: true` in production; when those retire, these
are the ones nobody counted.

**These are proposals with rationale, not applied changes.** They are contract
decisions, and the contract is being regenerated by another session as this is
written — applying them now would collide. Everything below cites the contract
or the client source rather than asserting.

## CANONICALIZE — 17 routes · real gaps in the v1 surface

### `/api/support/*` — 10 routes, the largest single gap

`GET /support/tickets` · `GET /support/tickets/:id` · `GET /support/unread-count` ·
`POST /support/tickets/:id/replies` · `/close` · `/reopen` · `/mark-read` ·
`GET /support/safety/incidents` · `POST /support/safety/incidents` ·
`GET /support/safety/emergency-config`

v1 has only `bookings.supportCases.create` / `.list` — **booking-scoped**. A
customer support-ticket surface, an unread count and a **safety-incident
channel** have no canonical equivalent at all.

The safety routes deserve their own note: an emergency-config and
incident-reporting path is the surface a customer reaches when something has
gone wrong in person. It should not be the least-specified part of the API.

### Catalog-adjacent — 4 routes

`POST /api/quote` · `GET /api/services/:id/branches` ·
`GET /api/services/:id/coverage-geo` · `POST /api/services/:id/coverage-geo`

Pricing has no v1 surface at all, and quote is what a customer sees before
committing money. Coverage and branches are catalog-shaped and belong with
`catalog.*`.

### Booking-adjacent — 3 routes

`GET /api/booking/:id/provider` — "who is coming to this booking". v1 exposes
`providers/:providerUid/profile`, which needs a uid the client does not hold at
that point. Either the booking read carries the provider, or this needs a
canonical route.

`POST /api/branches/slots` — availability by branch. `provider.availability` is
provider-scoped and does not answer it.

`GET /api/user/registereduser` — an identity lookup; likely folds into
`identity.me`, but the query parameters need checking before that is asserted.

## ROLE_SPECIFIC — 3 routes · and a security question that outranks the disposition

| route | client method |
| --- | --- |
| `POST /api/:id/approve` | `approveGcashPayment` |
| `POST /api/:id/gcash-submit` | — |
| `POST /api/:id/mark-cash-paid` | — |

These are **payment settlement** actions. `ROLE_SPECIFIC` describes them — they
are the same domain service reached by a different role — but the disposition is
not the interesting part.

**The customer application holds `approve` and `mark-cash-paid`.** A prior audit
(SC-003) found both handlers skip the booking-ownership check, so any
authenticated user could mark any booking PAID. That was recorded as fixed in
the backend, but the *client* still carries the calls.

Recommendation, and it is a client change rather than a contract one: the
customer app should not hold a settlement call it is not entitled to make. It is
the same reasoning that keeps provider-side change-order creation out of this
client. Whether `gcash-submit` is genuinely customer-initiated should be settled
before it is classified.

## Summary

| disposition | count |
| --- | --- |
| KEEP | 6 |
| ALIAS_TEMPORARILY | 4 |
| CANONICALIZE | 17 |
| ROLE_SPECIFIC | 3 |

**17 of 30 are CANONICALIZE** — genuine gaps rather than migration debt. The v1
surface is missing customer support entirely, including the safety channel, and
has no pricing route.

That reframes the finding: this is not 30 routes waiting to be labelled. It is
one third labelling work, and two thirds a v1 surface that is less complete than
"105 of 105 implemented" suggests — because completeness was measured against
the contract, and the contract does not know these exist.
